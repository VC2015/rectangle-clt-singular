The common symmetric construction

Each symmetric summand has a Rademacher component and an independent component that is
activated rarely. The activation follows the construction in [22, Proposition E.1]. We use separate
indices j ≤ r and u ≤ m for the two components. This allows the number of leading coordinates,
which determines the Gaussian width, to differ from the number of coordinates over which the
rare shifts are maximized.
