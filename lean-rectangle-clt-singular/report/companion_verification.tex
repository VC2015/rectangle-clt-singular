\begin{abstract}
The separate Lean package \path{lean-rectangle-clt-singular/} proves the
five main theorems of the unrestricted-covariance companion manuscript
and both bounds in its sub-exponential corollary. These comprise three
bounded-observation upper bounds, the sub-exponential extension, an
explicit sharp lower construction, and the two full-class uniform
consistency criteria. The proofs use actual probability laws and
rectangle suprema. The upper bounds permit singular covariance and
every coordinate-rectangle endpoint convention; their final statements
have no unproved analytic estimate as a premise. This report records the
public statements, the newly included proof components, and the exact
checked sources and audits. Further standalone Supplement results and a
general Hausdorff surface or first-variation theorem remain outside its
scope.
\end{abstract}

\section{The proved statements}
Let $p,n\ge1$, and let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n$ be independent centered
$\R^p$-valued observations on a probability space, with
$\|\xi_i\|_\infty\le\delta$ almost surely. The observations need not
have identical laws; their coordinates may be dependent. Put
\[
 W=\sum_i\xi_i,\qquad \Sigma=\operatorname{Cov}(W),\qquad
 \Sigma_{jj}=1,\qquad Z\sim N(0,\Sigma).
\]
In Lean the Gaussian is the concrete pushforward \path{gaussianLaw A},
where $AA^{\mathsf T}=\Sigma$. An actual factor exists for every such
bounded array, including singular covariance, by
\path{gaussian_factor_exists_of_boundedIndependent}.

Write $\log_+x=\max\{0,\log x\}$ and
\[
 L=\log(ep),\qquad D_L=1+\log_+\frac1{\delta\sqrt L},\qquad
 c=1-\max_{j\ne k}|\Sigma_{jk}|,
\]
where the empty maximum is zero, giving $c=1$ for $p=1$. Define
\[
 \kappa=\begin{cases}\min\{L,c^{-1}\},&c>0,\\L,&c=0,\end{cases}
 \qquad E_\kappa=1+\log_+\frac{\sqrt\kappa}{\delta L}.
\]
For a measurable coordinate rectangle $R$, let
$\Delta_R=|\Prob(W\in R)-\Prob(Z\in R)|$.

\begin{theorem}[The three bounded CLTs]
There is one constant $C\ge1$, chosen before all dimensions, probability
spaces, distributions, and envelope parameters, such that for every $R$,
\begin{align}
 \Delta_R&\le C\min\{1,\delta L^{3/2}\sqrt{D_L}\},\label{eq:general}\\
 \Delta_R&\le C\min\{1,\delta[L^{3/2}+L\sqrt{\kappa E_\kappa}]\}.
 \label{eq:gap}
\end{align}
If the full signed aggregate third-moment tensor vanishes, that is,
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i\E[\xi_{ij}\xi_{ik}\xi_{i\ell}]=0
 \quad\text{for every }j,k,\ell,\label{eq:cancellation}
\end{equation}
then also
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_R\le C\min\{1,\delta L\sqrt{\kappa E_\kappa}\}.
 \label{eq:zero}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The formal bounds quantify over every \path{Rect p}: open, closed,
mixed, unbounded, and empty coordinate intervals are included. Atomic
observations are allowed. There is no positive eigenvalue, local
determinant, covariance-rank, or positive-gap assumption. Condition
\eqref{eq:cancellation} allows cancellation between observations and
includes mixed entries. Marginal third moments alone do not suffice.

\section{The analytic proof in Lean}
\paragraph{Concentration, selectors, and singular support.}
The Kane sensitivity formalization supplies bounded-sum rectangle
boundary concentration with constant $2^{30}$. The actual Gaussian
version has constant $12e^{3/2}$. Both allow arbitrary independent
measurable shifts, without moment assumptions on those shifts, and
permit singular covariance. The proof uses the smooth rectangle selector
\[
 F_s(x)=\left(1+\sum_{f\in\mathcal F}
       e^{(\langle n_f,x\rangle-b_f)/h}\right)^{-1},
 \qquad \theta_s=1-F_s,
\]
where $\mathcal F$ is a finite set of signed coordinate faces and $h>0$.
Actual entropy and log-sum inequalities bound the averaged selector means.
Pr\'ekopa--Leindler proves covariance contraction of the genuine Gaussian
selector tilt, including rectangular factors and repeated or omitted
faces. Support-coordinate constructions reconstruct the observations
almost surely for singular factors. Their covariance is a projection
$P\le I$; no inverse-eigenvalue factor enters the observable row bounds.

\paragraph{A signed Gaussian trace estimate.}
For a probability law $\nu$ of the base $V$, independent standard
$G\in\R^p$, a unit-row factor $A$, and $a\in\mathbb N$, the code uses
the actual power trace
\[
 T_a=\E\left[(\|G\|_2^2-p)
   F_s(V+\sigma AG)^{a+1}\theta_s(V+\sigma AG)\right].
\]
The quantitative input is the actual property
\path{UniformBoundaryConcentration}, with $C_0,\eta\ge0$, which bounds every translated
rectangle shell of width $d>0$ by
$C_0(d+\eta)\sqrt{L/v}$ under $\nu$. The checked theorem
\path{abs_averagedGaussianPowerTrace_le_concentration} gives, when
$v\ge1/4$, $\sigma>0$, and $\eta\sqrt L\le\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
 |T_a|\le
 C_{\rm surface}(C_0)\frac{hL\sqrt L}{a+1},\qquad
 C_{\rm surface}(C_0)=160e^{3/2}C_0(8e^{80}+16e^{136}).
 \label{eq:surface}
\end{equation}
This displays the true linear dependence on the input concentration
constant. The actual interpolation base supplies the fixed absolute
value $C_0=2^{30}+12e^{3/2}$, $\eta=2\delta$, and $v=1/4$.

The proof of \eqref{eq:surface} is a direct smooth proof. Gaussian
integration by parts produces facet and paired ridge contributions.
Gamma-clock random rectangles give exact rising-factorial factors
$(a+q)!/a!$ for $q=1,2$. Actual tube change-of-variables identities,
physical displacement bounds, and disjoint image events control the
contributions by boundary probabilities while retaining their Gaussian
tail factor. Genuine dyadic \texttt{HasSum} decompositions and concrete
Gaussian projection moments sum these bounds. The base law needs no
moment condition. This proves the required selector estimate without
asserting a full Hausdorff surface or first-variation theorem for
arbitrary polyhedra.

\paragraph{Common-base restoration and the actual path.}
Finite binomial expansion converts \eqref{eq:surface} to the signed
selector trace bound. Together with the actual mean and covariance
contraction estimates, it closes the averaged matrix estimate on the
full retained-array and split-Gaussian law. The translated selector
series converges under an explicit bandwidth condition. It permits
common-base restoration with genuine second moments and the support
covariance budget $P\le I$; it does not assume concentration conditional
on each mask.

The Bernoulli/Gaussian interpolation is an actual probability law.
Its derivative is computed, rather than posited as an analytic bound.
The exact fourth-order identity has a signed cubic term and three
remainder integrals with genuine product-law fresh copies. Measurability,
domination, Gaussian differentiation, and Fubini exchanges are checked.
Condition \eqref{eq:cancellation} removes the signed aggregate cubic
term before taking absolute values.

\paragraph{Time integration, endpoints, and optimization.}
The actual interpolation expectation is continuous through both
endpoints. Early and late derivative estimates are integrated with
switching time $\tau=Lh^2$; the inverse-time integral generates
\[
 \ell_h=\max\left\{0,\log\frac1{Lh^2}\right\}.
\]
The case $\tau\ge1$ is also covered. Sharp bounded/Gaussian endpoint
smoothing combines the covariance-free bound with the optional pair-gap
improvement. For every rectangle, the resulting checked estimates are
\begin{align}
 \Delta_R&\le M\left[\delta L\sqrt L+
       \frac{\delta^2L\sqrt L}{h}\ell_h+h\sqrt L\,\kappa\right],
       \label{eq:variational}\\
 \Delta_R&\le M\left[
       \frac{\delta^2L\sqrt L}{h}(1+\ell_h)+h\sqrt L\,\kappa\right]
       \quad\text{under \eqref{eq:cancellation}},\label{eq:variational-zero}
\end{align}
for $h\ge b\delta$. The proof transfers finite closed-box bounds to
all rectangle endpoints without requiring the bounded law to have a
density. Choosing
\[
 h=b\delta\sqrt{LE_\kappa/\kappa}
\]
is admissible and controls $\ell_h$. Scalar optimization gives
\eqref{eq:gap} and \eqref{eq:zero}, and $\kappa\le L$ and
$E_\kappa\le D_L$ give \eqref{eq:general}. Probability saturation
supplies the displayed $\min\{1,\cdot\}$ bounds.

\section{Sub-exponential observations}
The two bounds of Corollary~2 in the main manuscript are also proved.
Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n\in\R^p$ be independent and centered, with
\[
 \frac1n\sum_i\E X_{ij}^2=1,\qquad
 \max_{i,j}\|X_{ij}\|_{\psi_1}\le K,\qquad K\ge1,
 \qquad \|V\|_{\psi_1}=\inf\{t>0:\E e^{|V|/t}\le2\}.
\]
Put $W_n=n^{-1/2}\sum_iX_i$, let $Z_n$ have its exact Gaussian
covariance, and define
\[
 b_n=\frac{K\log(en)}{\sqrt n},\qquad
 D_n=1+\log_+\frac1{b_n\sqrt L},\qquad
 F_n=1+\log_+\frac1{b_nL}.
\]
Write $\rho_{\mathcal R}(U,V)$ for the supremum of the rectangle
probability differences. One universal constant gives
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\mathcal R}(W_n,Z_n)
 \le C\min\{1,b_nL^{3/2}\sqrt{D_n}\}.
 \label{eq:tail-general}
\end{equation}
If the correlation gap is at least a fixed $c_0>0$, then
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\mathcal R}(W_n,Z_n)
 \le C_{c_0}\min\{1,b_n[L^{3/2}+L\sqrt{F_n}]\}.
 \label{eq:tail-gap}
\end{equation}
The second constant depends only on $c_0$; the first requires no positive
gap or covariance eigenvalue. The observation laws need not be identical.

The public endpoints in \path{SubexponentialMain.lean} are
\path{subexponentialCLT_psi1} and
\path{fixedGapSubexponentialCLT_psi1}. Their hypotheses are the displayed
Orlicz bound and averaged second-moment normalization, rather than
assumed clipping or coupling estimates. The auxiliary APIs
\path{subexponentialCLT} and \path{fixedGapSubexponentialCLT} use a
genuine integrable coordinate exponential moment at scale $K$.
The extended-infimum definition of the Orlicz norm assigns infinity to
an empty admissible set. A norm bound at $K$ gives the exponential
moment bound at $2K$, without assuming that the infimum is attained;
the rate estimates absorb this change into the absolute constant.

\paragraph{Clipping and the discarded sum.}
The checked construction clips at $T=Ku$, where $u=4\log(en)$, and
centers both the clipped and residual rows. For one residual coordinate
$R_{ij}=X_{ij}-\operatorname{clip}_T(X_{ij})$, the proof gives
\[
 \E e^{|R_{ij}|/K}\le1+2e^{-u},\qquad
 \E R_{ij}^2\le4K^2e^{-u}.
\]
Independence across observations and Markov's inequality then yield
\[
 \Prob\!\left\{\left\|n^{-1/2}\sum_i(R_i-\E R_i)\right\|_\infty
       >\frac{K[s+4ne^{-u}]}{\sqrt n}\right\}\le pe^{-s}.
\]
Thus $s=\log(4np)$ controls the residual sum while the clipping
threshold depends only on $\log n$. The proof does not require that
all $np$ raw coordinates simultaneously lie below that threshold.
The relevant modules are \path{SubexponentialClipping},
\path{SubexponentialResidualMGF}, \path{SubexponentialBernstein}, and
\path{SubexponentialArray}.

\paragraph{Covariance and transfer.}
The \path{SubexponentialL2}, \path{SubexponentialGap}, and
\path{SubexponentialScaling} modules prove the actual variance and
correlation-gap perturbation estimates and positive diagonal
standardization. A jointly Gaussian pair has the block covariance of
the clipped and residual sums. Both cross blocks are retained, so its
sum has exactly the original covariance; independence between these two
Gaussian components is not assumed. The \path{SubexponentialGaussian},
\path{SubexponentialTransfer}, and \path{SubexponentialReduction}
modules supply Gaussian tails and two genuine coupling sandwiches.
The bounded CLTs and scalar rate estimates then prove
\eqref{eq:tail-general}--\eqref{eq:tail-gap} for every rectangle.
In particular, covariance restoration is now part of a completed
probabilistic proof, rather than only supporting matrix algebra.

\section{Lower scale and uniform consistency}
Theorems~4 and~5 of the main manuscript are proved with their full
construction, supremum, and limit conclusions. Their proof calculations
appear in the Supplement, but their inclusion in the certification is
determined by the following public statements.

\paragraph{The explicit lower construction.}
For arbitrary sequences $B_n\ge2$ and $p_n\to\infty$, suppose
\[
 r_n=\frac{B_n(\log p_n)^{3/2}}{\sqrt n}\longrightarrow0.
\]
The theorem constructs iid centered observations with independent
coordinates, covariance $I_{p_n}$, and attained envelope $B_n$, for
which
\begin{equation}
 \liminf_{n\to\infty}
 \frac{\rho_{\mathcal R}(W_n,Z_n)}{r_n}
 \ge\frac{\sqrt2}{4e},\qquad
 W_n=\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_iX_{n,i},\quad Z_n\sim N(0,I_{p_n}).
 \label{eq:lower-scale}
\end{equation}
Each scalar coordinate has the actual two-point law
\[
 \Prob(X=B_n)=\frac1{1+B_n^2},\qquad
 \Prob(X=-B_n^{-1})=\frac{B_n^2}{1+B_n^2}.
\]
The public theorem is \path{skewedTwoPointLowerScale} in
\path{LowerScaleMain.lean}. Its construction properties include iid row
laws, coordinate independence, both row and normalized-sum covariance,
and positive probability of attaining the envelope. No limit for
$1-B_n^{-2}$ or additional relation between $p_n$ and $n$ is imposed.
The conclusion uses the genuine supremum rectangle distance, with its
finite upper bound and the detecting-rectangle lower bound both proved.

The proof constructs the exponential tilt and proves Gaussian
approximation for the tilted centered array in
\path{LowerTiltedArray} and \path{LowerTiltedBerryEsseen}.
The likelihood identity and the two-point moderate-tail estimates are
proved in the \path{LowerChangeOfMeasure},
\path{LowerCramerNormalized}, and \path{LowerSkewTail} chain.
These estimates are uniform in the growing envelope. Independence then
gives actual maximum probabilities in \path{LowerMaxCDFActual};
\path{LowerRectangleDistance} and \path{LowerScaleMain} pass from a
detecting rectangle to \eqref{eq:lower-scale}. This is a proof for the
displayed two-point construction, not a claim that the Supplement's
more general moderate-tail lemma has been formalized for every bounded
scalar array.

\paragraph{The full-class consistency criteria.}
For fixed $c_0\in(0,1]$, let $M_n(c_0;B,p)$ be the supremum of
rectangle error over all independent centered observations with envelope
at most $B$, unit average marginal variances, and correlation gap at
least $c_0$. Let $M_n^0$ restrict this class by full signed aggregate
third-tensor cancellation. For arbitrary $B_n\ge2$ and $p_n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 M_n(c_0;B_n,p_n)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{B_n\{\log(ep_n)\}^{3/2}}{\sqrt n}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:consistency-general}
\end{equation}
For each fixed $B\ge1$ and arbitrary $p_n\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 M_n^0(c_0;B,p_n)\longrightarrow0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \frac{\log p_n}{\sqrt n}\longrightarrow0.
 \label{eq:consistency-zero}
\end{equation}
These are numerical suprema formed before taking the limit. The full
classes do not require identical observation laws, independent
coordinates, or positive covariance eigenvalues. The independent-coordinate
models used in the necessity proofs are members of these larger classes.

The individual endpoints are \path{fixedGapWorstError_iff_rate} and
\path{zeroSkewWorstError_iff_log_rate};
\path{uniformConsistencyTheorem} combines them in
\path{ConsistencyMain.lean}. The \path{UniformWorstError} module
defines the literal suprema and proves their equivalence to the uniform
probability-class statements. The \path{GeneralConsistency} chain
handles arbitrary growing envelopes. The \path{ZeroSkewConsistency}
chain includes the Rademacher fourth-order tail and critical-maximum
calculation: when $\log p_n\sim a\sqrt n$, $a>0$, the discrepancy
at the normal maximum threshold tends to
\[
 \exp\{-\exp(-a^2/3)\}-e^{-1}>0.
\]
The proofs then treat arbitrary dimension sequences, rather than only
sequences possessing this critical limit. These necessity arguments,
together with the upper bounds, give both directions of
\eqref{eq:consistency-general}--\eqref{eq:consistency-zero}.

\section{Scope and semantic checks}
The verified scope comprises the five main theorems and both bounds of
the sub-exponential corollary. Substituting $\xi_i=X_i/\sqrt n$ in the
bounded endpoints gives the normalized-observation corollary; a separate
wrapper is not needed for that implication. Positive diagonal scaling
is proved in the sub-exponential reduction. The sub-exponential APIs do
not assert a zero-skewness improvement: clipping need not preserve
cancellation between different rows, and their transfer estimate retains
a $b_nL^{3/2}$ term. A checked clipping example also shows that original
covariance minus clipped covariance need not be positive semidefinite.

Further standalone Supplement statements remain outside these
certificates. They include the general bounded-array moderate-tail
lemma; exact coordinate collapse along equal or opposite coordinates,
with its rectangle-distance and rank identities; standalone rank-one
approximation and minimax statements; singular lower-bound embeddings;
and the two-face curvature result. The optional signed-remainder and
time-primitive investigations
are not additional certified sharp CLTs. The general Hausdorff surface
and first-variation theorem discussed above is likewise not asserted.
The supporting two-point and Rademacher calculations are included in
the certified main lower and consistency proofs.

Two finite probability examples distinguish the hypotheses. Independent
symmetric signs $X,Y$ give a bounded vector
$(X,Y,(3X+4Y)/5)$ with covariance
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0&3/5\\0&1&4/5\\3/5&4/5&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad c=1/5,\qquad a_3=a_4=0.
\]
The nonzero null vector is $(-3/5,-4/5,1)$. This model is covered by
the companion but fails the positive local four-coordinate eigenvalue
assumption of the earlier local theorem. Separately, $(X,Y,XY)$ has
zero marginal third moments but $\E[X\,Y\,(XY)]=1$, so it does not
satisfy full tensor cancellation.
